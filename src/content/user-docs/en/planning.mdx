# Cash Flow projection

Cash Flow builds a liquidity calendar. It starts from the latest known cash balances, adds commitments in the cycle, and calculates the balance after each date. It is not a statistical forecast of behavior; it is the result of known items.

## The cycle

Preferences set frequency, count, and a start date or day of month. Navigate previous, current, and next cycles. The cycle changes the displayed window, not the original item dates.

## Row sources

Cash Flow combines planned expenses and income, active subscriptions, installments, card statements, planned transfers, and one-off scheduled transactions recorded in the ledger. The ledger mirrors only the next unpaid occurrence of each recurring planned item; Cash Flow continues to list every occurrence in the selected cycle. A planned rule keeps its optional memo across those occurrences, and the Cash Flow edit dialog exposes the payee and memo separately. Money-box funding needs are shown separately and do not change an account balance by themselves. A reconciled or paid occurrence remains identifiable but must not be applied twice.

A scheduled ledger movement is applied to the projection on its future date even though it has not moved today's account balance. Scheduled outflows also count as commitments in **Safe to spend**; scheduled inflows remain expected income and do not increase the main Safe-to-spend figure before they arrive.

<CalculationBreakdown title="Account series">
  `balance after row = balance before + inflow − outflow`.

For an account with overdraft, projected availability receives the same movement. The first value below zero sets that account's first negative date.

If balance is absent or item currency differs, the row can remain visible while its series is marked not projected.

</CalculationBreakdown>

<CalculationBreakdown title="Aggregate series">
  Only opening balances for cash accounts in the selected primary currency are added, followed by their dated rows. CARD
  accounts never reduce aggregate liquidity or raise negative-cash alerts: a negative CARD balance is debt, not a cash
  deficit.
</CalculationBreakdown>

## Unassigned account

An expense or subscription without an account, or a card statement without a settlement account, appears as unassigned. It remains a known commitment, but Tracky cannot know which balance it reduces. Assign the source account for a complete projection.

## Three complete scenarios

### 1. Ordinary month

Opening balance `180000` minor units. Salary `CRDT 220000` on the 27th, rent `DBIT 90000` on the 2nd, subscriptions `15000`. In date order: `180000 − 90000 − 15000 + 220000 = 295000`. A cycle starting on the 25th places salary before expenses; one starting on the 1st applies expenses first.

### 2. Card statement

CARD balance is `-75000`; settlement account starts at `120000`. Closing creates a `75000` statement on the current account, projecting it to `45000`. CARD is not added to cash. Once current-account `DBIT` and CARD `CRDT` are matched, the cycle becomes paid and is not applied again.

### 3. Installments plus a scheduled movement

Opening `100000`; loan installment `25000`, one-off scheduled outflow `60000`, and subscription `20000`. Balances become `75000`, `15000`, then `-5000`: the subscription date is first negative. Moving an item in Tracky is valid only when the real due date changes; otherwise add real income or reduce an outflow.

## Reading an alert

Open the reported day, inspect all earlier rows, and verify opening balance, currency, and account. A negative notification uses the earliest affected group date and clears only when source data produces a non-negative series.

<Callout title="Cash Flow does not move money">
  Creating a transfer, money box, due item, or scheduled transaction records intent. You must still perform the real
  operation with your bank or creditor.
</Callout>
