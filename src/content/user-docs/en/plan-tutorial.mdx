# Plan walkthrough

This guide is the practical companion to [Plan, categories and subscriptions](/app/docs/plan-and-subscriptions).
That page tells you what each control does; this one tells you **in which order to use them**, and why.

## The idea in one sentence

The Plan does not ask how much you would like to spend: it asks you to **give a job to the money you already
have**. Every euro sitting in the plan's accounts is assigned to a bucket until *Ready to Assign* reaches
zero. It is not a forecast, it is a decision taken over money that exists.

## Start on the day the Plan becomes real

Every Plan has a starting day. A new Plan normally starts on the day you create it: earlier movements remain
part of today's account balances, but they do not create *Activity* or overspending. The first month therefore
describes only the decisions you could actually have made after the Plan existed.

If you imported history or chose the wrong origin, open the plan selector and use **Restart from here…**.
Choose the first day that should count. Tracky keeps the Plan's groups, buckets, targets, and assignments, but
rebuilds its history and stops treating earlier spending as overspending.

The Plan still starts from **today's real cash balances**, not a reconstructed opening balance. The chosen
date decides which movements explain bucket activity; it does not invent money that is no longer in the
accounts.

## Negative Ready to Assign: three different causes

The label above the number changes with the reason, because the remedies differ.

**The cash accounts are overdrawn.** The arranged overdraft is borrowed capacity, not money to assign. The
notice at the top shows the amount, remaining facility, change since last month, and—after you choose a
date—the monthly pace toward zero. Do not create a payoff bucket: every euro of future income you leave
unassigned already stays in the account and reduces the negative balance.

**You assigned more than you have.** Lower an assignment, or move money between buckets from the inspector:
the total does not change, only who holds it.

**Categories hold more than the liquidity.** This follows cash overspending in the previous month, which
reduces the new month's *Ready to Assign* instead of staying in the category.

## Cash overspending and credit overspending

The two figures shown on a red row need different action.

**Cash overspending** means the money has already left a cash account. Move Available money from another
bucket to cover it. If you do not, the shortfall lowers *Ready to Assign* rather than carrying as a negative
bucket balance.

**Credit overspending** means the purchase was made on a card without enough money in its spending bucket.
No cash left yet, but the card debt increased. Fund the card's payment bucket so that cash is ready when the
statement is paid.

## Targets: the part that changes everything

A target states **how much a bucket should receive and by when**. It does two jobs: telling you what is
missing this month, and ordering priorities when there is not enough money to go around.

A repeating **Custom** target can run every N months or years. Use every three months for a quarterly bill,
for example, or every two years for a renewal that does not fit the monthly or yearly cadences.

### Recurring monthly spending

Groceries, dining, transport, fuel. Cadence **Monthly**, behaviour **Refill up to**. What you do not spend
lowers next month's need, and the target behaves like a cap. If instead you want the unspent part to
accumulate — a gifts fund, say — use **Set aside another**.

### Annual bills: use the yearly cadence, not your own division

This is the question everyone asks: *insurance costs €600 once a year, should I set a €50 monthly target?*

Arithmetically it works, but it is a dead number: it asks for €50 whatever happens. Skip a month and you
arrive short without noticing. Start in October for a March due date and €50 a month is not enough.

**Use the Yearly cadence with the due date instead, and the *Refill up to* behaviour.**
Tracky computes this month's need as:

```
(full amount − what you already set aside) ÷ months left until the due date
```

The division is redone every month. Skipped August? September automatically asks for more. Starting
mid-year? It spreads over the time that actually remains. And as the bucket fills, the need falls by itself.

**Mind the behaviour.** With *Set aside another* the subtraction of what you already saved does **not**
happen: the need becomes `full amount ÷ months remaining`, so it grows month after month while the fund
fills, and you would set aside far more than the premium. On €600 due in March, in July both ask for
€66.67; in October, with €200 already saved, *Refill up to* still asks €66.67 while *Set aside another*
asks €100. For annual bills always use *Refill up to*.

This is what YNAB calls a **true expense**: an annual bill stops being a surprise and becomes a monthly
instalment you pay to yourself. It applies to insurance, road tax, servicing, taxes, annual subscriptions.

### Bills spread across the year: service charges and the like

Building service charges look like neither the weekly shop nor the insurance policy. They are **several
payments spread through the year** — typically four quarterly instalments — plus balancing charges and extra
levies you cannot predict to the cent, but with a **fairly stable annual total**.

Here the yearly cadence is the wrong choice, even though the expense is annual in total. The fund empties at
every instalment, so what you have set aside falls, and with the yearly formula the monthly ask would **rise
again** right after each payment: it would fight itself.

Use **Monthly** with **Set aside another…** and the annual total divided by twelve. The fund grows in the
months without an instalment and partly empties at each due date, which is exactly what you want.

**Mind the reversal.** On the monthly cadence the two behaviours do the opposite of what they do on the
yearly one: *Set aside another* asks for the full amount **every month regardless of what is already put
away**, so it accumulates; *Refill up to* stops as soon as the bucket reaches the threshold, so it caps. For
an accrual you want the first, for a spending cap the second. The real rule is not the word "annual" but
**how the payments are spread**: one payment on a known date wants the yearly cadence with *Refill up to*,
several payments scattered through the year want the monthly one with *Set aside another*.

For the amount, look at the actual year-end total rather than the nominal instalment: four instalments of
€428.89 come to €1,715, but with balancing charges and extra levies the real total can reach €1,833. Divide
that, rounding up, so the extras do not catch you short.

### Two similar bills with different cadences

Two insurance policies — the car charged monthly, the motorbike paid once a year — want **two categories**,
and that is not an interface quirk: a target lives on a bucket, and a bucket has exactly one target.
Different cadences mean different targets, so different buckets, so different categories.

Put the car on a bucket with a monthly *Refill up to* target, and the motorbike on a bucket with a yearly
*Refill up to* target and the renewal date. That way you see separately what you spend each month and what
you are accruing towards the due date — which is exactly why keeping them apart is worth it.

The same holds when the spending is "the same kind of thing" but the bucket would blur it: keeping service
charges inside *Housing* makes that bucket's *Available* unreadable, because you can no longer tell the fund
that is accruing from the slack left for other home costs. A dedicated category, and the number says one
thing again.

### Snoozing a target

For a month where that spending makes no sense — holidays in January — snooze the target rather than
deleting it. It leaves the month's underfunded list without losing its configuration.

## Cost to Be Me

It sums every target for the month and compares it with the **expected income** you entered. It is the cost
of a fully funded month of your life.

Without expected income the card only shows the target total and tells you nothing. With that number in
place it answers the real question: *does my lifestyle fit inside my income?* If the targets exceed the
income, no assignment will fix it — the problem is upstream.

**Expected income belongs to the plan, not to a month.** You enter it once and it stays as you move between
months: it describes a typical month, not July or August in particular. That is why it wants your
**recurring, reliable** income — take-home pay — and not a one-off sum, which compared against targets that
return every month would be a false comparison. If your income varies, use the **lowest amount you can count
on** rather than the average: underestimating leaves you extra money to assign, overestimating has you
planning money that never arrives.

A windfall — a tax refund, back pay, a gift — does not belong here. When it actually lands, the Plan sees it
as income and it shows up in *Ready to Assign*: you give it a job then. That is the difference between
planning the money you have and planning the money you hope for.

Six strategies, all of them previewed before you confirm:

**Underfunded** is the one you will use most. It spreads the available money starting with what is already
in the red, then targets with a near due date, then the rest. It is the right way to use a small amount.

**Assigned last month** and **Spent last month** replay the previous month: useful when life is stable and
you want to start quickly.

**Average assigned** looks further back and smooths the peaks.

**Reset assigned** and **Reset available** start over: the first zeroes the month's assignments, the second
also empties the carry.

Leave every bucket unchecked to apply the strategy to all visible ones, or tick a few to act only on those.

## Unplanned, and why it is good news

The *Unplanned* row collects activity from categories no bucket covers. It is not an error: it is where you
discover that the plan does not yet describe your whole life.

When you see one, use **Assign category** on that row: put it into an existing bucket or give it one of its
own. From then on that spending has a row of its own.

Categories you create from Settings join the plan automatically, so this mostly shows up for older ones or
for categories created before the plan existed.

## Card and installment buckets: prepare the payment once

Each card in the Plan has its own protected payment bucket. When a categorized card purchase is covered by
money in its spending bucket, Tracky shows that amount as **Covered spending** and makes it available for the
future card payment. Open the Activity details to see covered purchases separately from statement payments.

When the statement debit appears on the settlement account, open the card bucket and choose **Register
statement payment**. Select that debit and confirm. Tracky creates the missing movement on the card and links
the two sides. The original purchases remain the expenses; paying the statement only consumes the cash that
was reserved for them.

Every ordinary active installment plan also has a protected bucket with the current month's installment. A loan
paired with one of your categories uses that category instead, without a second Instalments row. This makes the
cash requirement visible even when the financed purchase happened earlier or outside the Plan. When a
card balance is converted into installments, that remaining installment debt is subtracted from the card
bucket's need: you fund the installments, not the same principal twice.

Card and installment buckets use the full derived need by default. Add a target if you want a different
pace, such as giving the card €400 each month.

## Plan or Goals: which one does the setting aside

The Plan and Goals both let you put money aside, so it is easy to end up doing it twice for the same thing
and seeing two different figures telling you what is still missing.

Goals are not redundant: they feed the Cash Flow projection and the *safe to spend* figure on the
Dashboard, neither of which the Plan provides. What overlaps is only the monthly accrual towards a purpose.

The rule is not about which feature you prefer, but **what kind of expense it is**:

A **recurring bill** — service charges, insurance, road tax — returns every year and is part of what a month
of your life costs. It belongs in the **Plan**, as a bucket with a target, so that it reaches Cost to Be Me.
Leaving it only in Goals makes that figure say your month costs less than it does.

A **one-off goal** — a gift, a trip, an emergency fund — is reached and then spent. It is not a recurring
monthly cost: it belongs in **Goals**.

If a money box already holds savings for an ordinary Plan bucket, link it from that bucket's inspector.
Tracky shows the amount as **Already set aside** and asks only for the target remainder. It is not a new
assignment, and it is read fresh each month instead of being carried again, so the same savings do not
silently double.

**Something to know if you use your bank's pockets or vaults.** The Plan reads liquidity from the **balances
the accounts report**. Some banks report the main balance **net of** pockets: in that case the money moved
there is outside the Plan's cash liquidity even if you link the matching money box. The link can cover that
bucket's target, but it does not turn the pocket into assignable cash.

## Being a month ahead

This is the goal of the method, and it is worth naming explicitly because everything else leads here.

While you assign money that arrives *during* the month, you are always catching up: spending comes before
decisions. Once you can cover a whole month with money earned in the previous one, on the first of the month
you sit down, assign everything, and for thirty days you no longer have to wonder whether you can afford
something — you already decided.

It is not reached in one go. Every euro left at the end of a month and not spent is a step.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I choose cadence and behaviour, briefly?**
Look at how the payments are spread, not at what the expense is called. You spend it every month and want a
cap: **Monthly + Refill up to**. You pay it once a year on a known date: **Yearly + Refill up to**, with the
due date. You pay it in instalments scattered through the year with a stable total: **Monthly + Set aside
another**, for the annual total divided by twelve. It is a one-off goal that then ends: **Custom + Have a
balance of**.

**Do I have to assign down to zero?**
It is the goal, not an obligation. A positive *Ready to Assign* means you hold money without a job: sooner
or later it leaves on its own, on something you did not choose.

**What happens if I spend more than a category holds?**
The bucket goes red. For cash overspending, move money from another bucket; otherwise the shortfall lowers
*Ready to Assign*. For credit overspending, fund the card payment bucket so you can settle the debt later.
The negative category balance itself does not carry into the next month.

**Does unspent money stay in the category?**
Yes, a positive *Available* carries into the next month. That is how funds for annual bills are built.

**Why doesn't my salary appear in a category?**
Income is not category activity: it funds the plan. You find it in *Ready to Assign* and its breakdown,
never as negative spending inside a bucket.

**What should Unexplained movement show?**
Zero. Income and every other component are counted directly. A non-zero value means some account movement
has not been attributed correctly, not that you earned or spent extra money.

**I recategorized an old transaction — does the plan update?**
Yes. The last two months are always recomputed live, and edits to older months invalidate the cache on their
own. *Recalculate plan* remains a safety net, not the normal path.

**Can I have more than one plan?**
The first one is for everybody. Additional plans are a Pro feature — useful to separate perimeters, for
example personal accounts from a business, or a second currency.

**Does hiding a category remove its money from the plan?**
No. Hiding only changes what you see: that bucket's *Available* still counts in the totals and in *Ready to
Assign*. The *Hidden categories* view brings it back.
